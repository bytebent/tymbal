## Read in full

| Source | What tymbal takes from it |
|---|---|
| Bennet-Clark 1997, *Tymbal mechanics and the control of song frequency in the cicada Cyclochila australasiae*, J. Exp. Biol. 200:1681–1694, [doi](https://doi.org/10.1242/jeb.200.11.1681) | Four ribs buckle posterior to anterior. Each IN pulse: a rarefaction building over about 20 µs, a larger compression, then near-exponential decay; the first 2–3 cycles are nonlinear and the frequency rises 150–350 Hz through the decay. Table 2 (unloaded tymbal): ribs 1–4 at 4.37, 4.19, 3.92, 3.17 kHz with levels 1, 1.01, 0.83, 0.39; OUT 6.54 kHz at 0.34. A 380 µg load lowers all of them (×0.74–0.82 IN, ×0.68 OUT). Q about 10 IN, 6 OUT, rising as frequency falls (Q ∝ 1/f, Fig. 6B); the loaded tymbal in the intact insect has about half the unloaded Q. Coherence: a rib that follows while the last pulse is above about ⅓ of its peak starts on its inward half-cycle; below about ¼ phase-locking breaks down; by 1/10 it is lost (the abstract gives 1/10). In song, ribs buckle 2–3 cycles apart, about 0.5–0.7 ms. Forces: 0.25 N to buckle, 0.05 N to hold; energy is stored over 2–4 ms and released in one cycle (about 230 µs), a tenfold power gain. Masses: plate about 550 µg wet, ribs 50–90 µg dry. Tymbal resonance at rest 5.4–7 kHz. |
| Young & Bennet-Clark 1995, *The role of the tymbal in cicada sound production*, J. Exp. Biol. 198:1001–1019, [doi](https://doi.org/10.1242/jeb.198.4.1001) | The tymbal itself is a resonator: about 4 kHz buckled in (sealed and open preparations, no air sac), about 5.5–6 kHz at rest; pushed in gradually it goes from 5.7 to about 4 kHz. At rest the tymbal is 12–24 dB more opaque at the song frequency, so it does not drain the other tymbal's pressure. Q: tymbal 9.3, air sac 3.4, both together 12.5 (Table 1); unloaded tymbal 13–20 (12.9 sealed, 19.9 open, 16.8 driven). Muscle-driven in-click in the intact insect Q 4–5; out-click over 20 dB quieter, around 5.7–6 kHz. Envelope modulation at cycles 1, 3 and 5: ribs 1, 2 and 3 two cycles apart. Up to three ribs buckle in protest song (video); two or three per movement. Left and right tymbals alternate, the OUT of one coinciding with the IN of the other; they can differ (one pulse Q 13, the other ragged with a 5.4 kHz peak). In calling song the 6 kHz components are at least 30 dB below the 4 kHz peak. Volume displaced about 6 µl into a 2 ml air sac; internal peaks 155–159 dB SPL, 148.5 dB outside the eardrums. Muscle about 120 Hz per tymbal. |
| Bennet-Clark & Young 1992, *A model of the mechanism of sound production in cicadas*, J. Exp. Biol. 173:123–153, [doi](https://doi.org/10.1242/jeb.173.1.123) | Helmholtz model: f₀ = (c/2π)√(A/(L·V)), A both eardrums, L = 16r/3π, r the equivalent radius of one. *Cyclochila*: V 1.93 ml, A 86 mm², r 3.7 mm, L 6.3 mm → 4.56 kHz against a 4.3 kHz song; *Macrotristria*: 4.41 against 4.0 kHz; *M. cassini* 6.7 against 6 kHz; *M. septendecim* 4.4 predicted against 1.3 kHz sung, explained by thick eardrums (covering the model's holes with rubber or paper: 4.3 → 1–2.4 kHz). Calculated body Q 5.5 (*Cyclochila*), 7.0 (*Macrotristria*); measured bodies 5.5–7.6; 1.8 ml model 6.5–7.8. Song pulses: Q 12–19 (slow-decaying) or 5.4–7 (fast). A click of Q 5.6 into the model gives Q 13, about the sum. Opercula on the model: removed 4.33 kHz, 65.6 dB, Q 7.8; 5 mm gap 3.93 kHz, 67.2 dB, Q 8.4; 0.5–1 mm gap 3.54 kHz, 56.2 dB, Q 5.2. Dead bodies: 3.7 → 4.7 kHz when opened. Coherent clicks 450 µs apart build one long pulse; clicks out of phase (760 µs) give a double-humped envelope; sidebands at f₀ ± 1/(click interval). Higher cavity modes: about 15 dB down in short cavities, as strong as the fundamental in long ones; weak resonances near 7.5 and 9 kHz above 4.3 kHz; a partition inside makes the tone less pure. A Helmholtz resonator acts as a low-pass above about 2f₀. Ideal pulse: about 5 cycles build, 5 plateau, 5 decay. |
| Young & Josephson 1983, *Pure-tone songs in cicadas with special reference to the genus Magicicada*, J. Comp. Physiol. 152:197–207, [doi](https://doi.org/10.1007/BF00611184) | *M. cassini*: about 9 sub-pulses per pulse from 9 ribs, Q 5 around 6 kHz, a small peak near 1 kHz (sub-pulse rate); phrase starts at 15–30 pulses/s then 170–200 (mean 180); muscles alternate at 90 Hz. *M. septendecim*: unbroken pure tone at 1.3 kHz, Q 25, harmonic at 2.6 kHz 30 dB down; amplitude modulation at 120–150 Hz (mean 136), each muscle at 68 Hz; phrases of 2–3 s with a drop in level and pitch at the end, about 1 s apart, about three times. Both have 11–12 ribs. Sub-pulse rate rises from about 650 Hz at 20 °C to 1200 Hz at 35 °C while the carrier stays fixed; *septendecim*'s carrier stays just over 1 kHz from 20 to 35 °C. Twitch rise time 7.6 → 5.2 ms (*cassini*) and 9.2 → 6.4 ms (*septendecim*) from 25 to 35 °C. About 5 ms from muscle action potential to sound. *septendecim*'s tymbal is much less stiff; the proposed mechanism: a slack tymbal lets the resonator's phase set the exact moment of each buckle. *Cystosoma* song Q 15. |
| Bennet-Clark & Young 1998, *Sound radiation by the bladder cicada Cystosoma saundersii*, J. Exp. Biol. 201:701–715, [doi](https://doi.org/10.1242/jeb.201.5.701) | Song about 850 Hz, bimodal pulses of 20–25 ms, Q 16.5–17. The tymbals overlap: the second starts 5–7 ms after the first (a quarter of the cycle at the motor neurone level). Seven ribs. Muscle-driven: rib 1 at 1.03–1.11 kHz, ribs 2 and 3 at 830–870 Hz and 4–6 dB louder, rib 4 at 630–750 Hz; rib 2 4–6 ms after rib 1, rib 3 straight after; OUT at 1.6–1.9 kHz about 20–30 ms after the start. Hand-pulled ribs: Q 14, 15, 17.1 for ribs 1–3. Abdomen extended from 32–34 to 39–42 mm: amplitude doubles, Q 4 → 9; frequency 815 Hz at 34 mm, 900 Hz at 41 mm. Frequency ∝ 1/√volume and 1/√mass. Radiators: both sides of tergites 3–5 and the sternites, all in phase, the sternites 10 dB lower over about three times the area; loosely coupled, they can ring at separate frequencies when loaded unevenly. Air sac 5.6–9.6 ml. Model: f = (1/2π)√(S²ρc²/(m·V)). |
